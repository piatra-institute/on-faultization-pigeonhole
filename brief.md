# Editorial brief

Question: what do failures of a small placement model reveal about its admission
information, cost comparison and performance measures?

Contribution: separate complete placement, resource coverage and load balance;
identify the closed-hole trap; isolate tiny-noise encoding and decision-boundary
effects; distinguish occupancy-preserving closure from eviction.

Reader: someone working on distributed allocation or perturbation experiments
who wants an inspectable mechanism rather than a taxonomy of competencies.

Strongest alternative explanation: every result is generated by the specified
transition rules and reports. The mathematical bound is a constraint, not an
extra source of allocation work. The revised paper adopts that explanation.

Structure: concrete refusal; measurement and move identity; paired methods;
admission results; report corruption; dynamic events and scope. No numbered
novelty list, Classification codas or repeated philosophical recap.

Voice: direct descriptions, equations where they settle a point, and explicit
judgments about what was measured. Keep uncertainty next to the relevant result.
Avoid personifying the items or presenting a score excursion as an intention.

Scope: exploratory ten-item/seven-hole study. Algebraic identities generalize
under their stated assumptions; finite-run effect sizes do not. No biological,
worker-policy, consciousness or ontological conclusion is established here.
